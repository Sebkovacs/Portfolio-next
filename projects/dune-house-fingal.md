Nestled gently between native spinifex grasses and coastal tea-trees in Fingal Bay, Dune House responds directly to the delicate ecology and dynamic winds of the Port Stephens coastline. 

The building form sits deliberately low to minimize visual impact from the public beach, while sweeping curved Sydney sandstone blade walls protect private courtyard zones from onshore salt squalls and anchor the dwelling into the shifting topography.

### Ecological Integration & Form
The design language is informed by the aerodynamics of wind and sand. The roofline dips and rises in response to prevailing coastal vectors, deflecting harsh winds over the structure while scooping gentle summer breezes into the central courtyard. This creates a sheltered microclimate that extends outdoor living into the fringe seasons.

### Material Philosophy: Resistance & Camouflage
The material palette was chosen for absolute minimal maintenance and visual subservience to the landscape.
- **Charred Timber (Shou Sugi Ban):** The carbonized cedar cladding delivers extraordinary resistance to salt spray, fire embers, and rot without synthetic sealants or chemical maintenance. It visually recedes into the shadows of the dune scrub.
- **Dry-Stack Sandstone:** Sourced locally, the sandstone spines provide immense thermal mass and acoustic isolation from the roaring ocean.

### Sustainable & Place-Responsive Innovations
- **Topographical Sensitivity:** The entire structure is elevated on discrete screw-piles to protect root zones and allow natural groundwater flow and sand movement to continue uninterrupted beneath the living floor.
- **Microclimate Courtyards:** Central sheltered outdoor deck provides sun-drenched, wind-protected living even during strong offshore or southerly weather events.
- **Water & Energy Autonomy:** A 30,000L sub-deck water retention bladder and a 12kW concealed solar array provide complete water and energy resilience for true off-grid capability.
