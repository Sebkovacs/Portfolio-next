### The Problem
Maximizing panoramic ocean views on a highly exposed, salt-battered headland cliff presents a significant structural and material challenge. The architecture must simultaneously act as a protective bunker against southerly gales and open up expansively to the horizon, all while maintaining privacy from adjacent cliff-side public pathways.

### Client Profile
A retired ocean-swimmer and local surf club stalwart who has spent his entire life in the water. He sought a permanent residence that felt like an extension of the cliff face—a robust, low-maintenance home where he could watch the swell lines roll in year-round.

### Sculptural Resilience
Headland House rejects lightweight coastal construction in favor of sheer mass. It is a building designed to weather and endure.

- **Material Defenses:** The primary structural shell is cast from marine-grade off-form concrete, paired with standing-seam zinc roofing. Both materials will naturally patina, blending the home into the grey tones of the cliffside rock over time.
- **Strategic Fenestration:** While the street-facing and southern facades are heavily fortified with minimal openings, the eastern facade completely dissolves. Deep, angled concrete reveals protect the expansive ocean-facing glass from driving rain and intense summer sun.
- **Thermal Mass & Stability:** The heavy concrete construction provides immense thermal mass, buffering the interior temperature against the rapid weather changes typical of the exposed headland.
